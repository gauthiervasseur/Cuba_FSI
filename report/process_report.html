Cuba · Unión Eléctrica daily notes · updated 29 October 2026

How the Cuba grid dataset was built

Every morning the Unión Eléctrica (UNE) publishes a note: yesterday's worst shortfall, the situation at dawn, and tonight's forecast deficit. State outlets republish it. This page traces how those notes became a daily table, what failed along the way, and how far the numbers can be trusted.

01 · Find and fetch

Where the notes came from

The plan was to read Cubadebate's own site first and fill gaps elsewhere. Cubadebate refused every automated connection, so the archive copies kept by the Internet Archive became the main source. Its index can be searched by web address, and Cubadebate dates each article in the address, so every month from 2018 onward could be listed and fetched.

From 2018 to 2020 the archive holds 52 UNE articles but no daily notes in this form. The first one found is dated 16 March 2021; publication becomes daily in June 2022.

03 · Read

Two readers, every note

Each note covers three moments, and the same word ("afectación") means something different in each. The extraction keeps them apart.

Yesterday

Highest shortfall and its time, whether the deficit lasted all day, when service was restored.

This morning

Snapshot time, available capacity, demand and the load cut at that moment; expected shortfall at midday.

Tonight's peak

Forecast availability, demand, deficit and the load that will be cut. Causes: plant breakdowns, maintenance, fuel shortages, solar output.

Claude Haiku 5.5

  • Reads the whole note and fills a strict schema of 23 figures plus lists of broken and maintained units.
  • Returns every figure with the exact words it came from, so each value can be checked against the text.
  • Prompt version 4; about $2 for a full run over all 1,571 notes.

Text patterns

  • Written by hand, with no model involved, for the 11 core figures.
  • Its only job is to disagree when the model is wrong.
  • Reads only within the sentence that names the figure, so it cannot borrow a number from the next one.

04 · Check and re-read

What sends a note to review

Every figure goes through the same checks. Some failures mean the reading is suspect; others mean the reading is right and the note itself is inconsistent.

Sends the note to review

quote not in text
the quoted words do not appear in the note
value not in quote
the number is not in its own quote
patterns disagree
the second reader found a different number
out of range
outside plausible bounds, e.g. demand above 4,200 MW
arithmetic
availability minus demand does not give the deficit
bad time
a time that cannot exist or is out of order
weekday
the weekday named does not match the date

Kept, with a note

source arithmetic
both readers agree, but the UNE's own sums do not add up (16 days)
paraphrased quote
the model reworded the quote, but the patterns confirm the value
snapshot vs gap
the morning cut differs from demand minus availability (9 days)

Haiku's first pass flagged 78 notes. Claude Sonnet 5.5, a larger model, re-read only those. Where the two models gave different figures, each disagreement was checked against the note by hand.

05 · Score

How accurate is it?

The model was scored against a reference set of 27 notes from October 2024, with each figure labelled separately.

100%Haiku 5.5, on all 23 figures
96%Text patterns on yesterday's maximum and its time (one missed); 100% on the other nine
150notes sampled for a larger test, 25 per year 2021–2026, waiting to be labelled

Read the 100% with care. The 27 reference labels were written by Claude, not by a person, and come from a single month. They show the pipeline works on that month; they do not establish its error rate. Hand-labelling the 150-note sample is what will.

06 · Result

What the series shows

The figure most notes report the same way is yesterday's maximum shortfall. Its monthly average rose from under 500 MW in mid-2022 to over 2,000 MW in mid-2026.

In 2023 some notes give separate daytime and night-time maxima; the table keeps one, so those months may read low. Two days carry a grid-collapse flag (19 February 2023, 5 December 2024).

07 · Open items

What would make it stronger

  1. Label the 150-note sample by hand and score both models on it.
  2. Ask Cubadebate for access; its own site would close most of the 2026 gap.
  3. Split yesterday's maximum into day and night fields for the 2023 notes.
  4. Check the 50 flagged days by hand.
